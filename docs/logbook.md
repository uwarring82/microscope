## 2026-09-24 — Used vs new M4 mirror: backlit transmittance comparison (analysis, draft report)

- Data: a new mirror, the empty mirror mount and a previously used mirror of the same position type, all in one mirror mount under the same backlight, gain 50 and exposure series, at zoom marks 0.58, 2 and 4. Earlier backlit frames of the used mirror in a different holder, and one new-mirror series at gain 70, are excluded.
- Method (local `artifacts/captures/m4-comparison-20260924/analyze.py`): Bayer-plane relative transmittance T = (M − b)/(E − b) per pixel against the empty-mount frame at equal exposure. b is the exposure-dependent black offset from opaque mount areas: 9.9–13.9 DN, adopted uncertainty ±0.5 DN. Validity windows exclude saturated or dim reference pixels, and an exposure needs ≥90% valid pixels. The statistic is the median, cross-checked against an area-weighted ratio. The uncertainty is an empirical envelope: zoom/exposure half-range + black ±0.5 DN + reinsertion change, added linearly. Features are regions below 0.8 × local background, with 0.7/0.9 sensitivity cases.
- Result: used ÷ new = 0.571 green (±4.4%), 0.906 blue (±2.7%), 0.83 red (±16%; red mirror signal only 2–8 DN above black), consistent at all three zooms. Reinserting the new mirror changed its T by 1.5% (green). The reduction is uniform over the analysed areas: the used mirror's localized features cover 0.3–0.7% of the area at the main threshold (<2% at any threshold), and excluding them changes the median by <0.1%.
- Not established: cause, reflectance or loss at the operating wavelength, transmission spectrum, or whether both mirrors had the same coating when new. The quantity is band-integrated and specific to the unrecorded backlight and this camera. Recommended next: spectrometer transmission of both mirrors, power-meter reflectance at operating conditions against agreed criteria, provenance of the used mirror, and more reinsertions.

## 2026-09-30 — Display-lattice calibration: literature and uncertainty review

- Literature review, not new hardware evidence or a re-analysis of captures. Direct microscopy precedent: Szydlowski et al., *Cell phone digital microscopy using an oil droplet*, Biomedical Optics Express 11, 2328–2338 (2020), [DOI 10.1364/BOE.389345](https://doi.org/10.1364/BOE.389345), [open text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7249838/). The authors imaged a white iPhone Xs screen and used manufacturer screen dimensions/PPI and measured pixel separations to characterize droplet magnification.
- Close geometric-calibration precedent: Walk et al., *Smartphone screens as astrometric calibrators* (2023), [DOI 10.1142/S2251171723500095](https://doi.org/10.1142/S2251171723500095), [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04961). They used a Samsung S20 OLED grid, translated and rotated the screen to separate optical distortion from screen placement errors, and measured 189 ± 15 nm RMS non-linear pixel placement error. This is not a certification of absolute pitch and cannot be transferred to our iPhone. Related detector-testing work: Bottom et al. (2018), [Smartphone scene generator](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.06834), and Prod'homme et al. (2020), [smartphone-based arbitrary scene projector](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05025).
- Assessment: the basic phone-screen calibration technique has published precedent. Our implementation applies it to microscope scale, field dependence, camera-mode ratio and chromatic/focus checks using raw Bayer data. The 29 September hardware entry is newer than the single-setting status in `calibration-usaf.md` and `status.md`; its marked-zoom comparisons remain preliminary. The current working tree also includes a Fourier lattice helper and synthetic test for fine pitches; this review did not execute or change that code.
- Uncertainty qualification of the 26 September entry: 0.005% frame agreement measures repeatability; ±0.11% inferred from integer-PPI rounding is conditional on that rounding convention and does not bound manufacturing pitch error. The quoted ±0.15% total absolute uncertainty is therefore provisional, not a demonstrated accuracy or traceable calibration. Screen pitch/geometry, alignment, cover-glass/focus effects and reference-target uncertainty still need a supported budget. A single screen placement cannot independently separate screen geometry from optical distortion. Pending validation: comparison with a reference of known tolerance under matched conditions, and translated/rotated screen measurements. No camera access, new acquisition or tests in this documentation-only review.
