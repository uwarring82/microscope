## 2026-09-24 — Used vs new M4 mirror: backlit transmittance comparison (analysis, draft report)

- Data: a new mirror, the empty mirror mount and a previously used mirror of the same position type, all in one mirror mount under the same backlight, gain 50 and exposure series, at zoom marks 0.58, 2 and 4. The new mirror was reinserted at the end as a repeatability check, 22 minutes in total. The mount, arbitrary mirror rotation and the order of frames were confirmed by the operator. Earlier backlit frames of the used mirror in a different holder, and one new-mirror series at gain 70, are excluded.
- Method (local `artifacts/captures/m4-comparison-20260924/analyze.py`): Bayer-plane relative transmittance T = (M − b)/(E − b) per pixel against the empty-mount frame at equal exposure. b is the exposure-dependent black offset from opaque mount areas: 9.9–13.9 DN, adopted uncertainty ±0.5 DN. Validity windows exclude saturated or dim reference pixels, and an exposure needs ≥90% valid pixels. The statistic is the median, cross-checked against an area-weighted ratio. The uncertainty is an empirical envelope: zoom/exposure half-range + black ±0.5 DN + reinsertion change, added linearly. Features are regions below 0.8 × local background, with 0.7/0.9 sensitivity cases.
- Result: used ÷ new = 0.571 green (±4.4%), 0.906 blue (±2.7%), 0.83 red (±16%; red mirror signal only 2–8 DN above black), consistent at all three zooms. Reinserting the new mirror changed its T by 1.5% (green). The reduction is uniform over the analysed areas: the used mirror's localized features cover 0.3–0.7% of the area at the main threshold (<2% at any threshold), and excluding them changes the median by <0.1%.
- Not established: cause, reflectance or loss at the operating wavelength, transmission spectrum, or whether both mirrors had the same coating when new. The quantity is band-integrated and specific to the unrecorded backlight and this camera. Recommended next: spectrometer transmission of both mirrors, power-meter reflectance at operating conditions against agreed criteria, provenance of the used mirror, and more reinsertions.
